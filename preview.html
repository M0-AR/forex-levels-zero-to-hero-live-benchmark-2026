🌱 Beginner guide — read this and you are a professional

Let's work this out in a step-by-step way to be sure we have the right answer.
  1. What is a pip? The smallest price step (0.0001 on EUR/USD). On 1 lot it is ~$10; on 0.5 lots ~$5. That is the whole story's money math.
  2. What is leverage? Trading bigger than your cash. Bigger = faster both ways. 0.5 lots on $200 dies in 40 pips — NFP does that before coffee.
  3. What is NFP? US jobs report, first Friday monthly, 8:30am ET. Dollar jumps. Our live test: Fridays with NFP are +14 pips wider.
  4. What is expectancy? Average win × how often − average loss × how often. 56% × 1.5 − 44% × 1 = +0.40R. Positive = edge. Negative = donation.
  5. What is R? Your risk unit. Risk $10 to make $15 = 1.5R. Professionals speak R, not dollars — it removes emotion.
  6. What is drawdown? Fall from your peak. 100 → 92 = 8%. The 15% contract clause means investors can leave at −15%. Ours breaches only 1.3% of technician paths.
  7. What is Sharpe? Return per unit of wobble. >1 good, >2 very good, >3 suspicious. Ours: noise 0.29, real edge 1.32.
  8. What is spread/slippage/swap? The three tolls. Spread = buy/sell gap; slippage = price moved while clicking; swap = overnight rent. Together ≈ 1.625 pips/trade → 400%/yr at 20× leverage.
  9. What is B-book? Broker takes the other side instead of routing out. With the tolls above, the house wins the aggregate even at a fair coin. Our simulation: +$11.83/trade.
  10. What do I actually do? One pair, one session, risk 1%, take only clean setups, journal everything, never trade NFP at size. Boring = profitable.
